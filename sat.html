What is the SAT?

The SAT is a standardized test widely used for college admissions in the United States and internationally. It measures your readiness for college and provides colleges with a common data point to compare applicants.

The digital SAT is shorter, easier to take, and has more time per question. Your score demonstrates your abilities in reading, writing, and math – skills essential for college success.

Test Structure

📖 Reading & Writing

64 minutes (2 modules)
  • 54 questions total
  • 27 questions per module
  • Adaptive difficulty

🔢 Math

70 minutes (2 modules)
  • 44 questions total
  • 22 questions per module
  • Calculator allowed
  • Score: 200-800

Expert Tips for SAT Success

01

Start Early

Begin your SAT prep at least 3-6 months before your test date. Consistent daily practice yields better results than cramming.

02

Take Practice Tests

Complete full-length practice tests under timed conditions to build stamina and familiarize yourself with the digital format.

03

Learn from Mistakes

Review every wrong answer thoroughly. Understanding why you got something wrong is more valuable than getting it right.

04

Master Time Management

Practice pacing yourself. Know how much time to spend on each question to complete all sections comfortably.

05

Focus on Weaknesses

Identify your weak areas and dedicate extra time to improving them. Don't just practice what you're already good at.

06

Use Official Resources

Prioritize official College Board practice materials. They're the most accurate representation of the actual test.
